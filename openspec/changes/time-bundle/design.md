## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- One small, general job runner that feature 7 can reuse without changing it.
- A reminder is shown once, never twice, whatever restarts happen.
- One `when` shape still carries everything about a thought's date: the date, its repeat, and its reminders.
- A repeating thought is one thought, one row, one status.
- The feed token is never stored, logged, or sent anywhere but to the user who made it.

**Non-Goals:**

- Done for one occurrence of a repeating thought, and skipping or moving one occurrence (exceptions). Decision 6.
- Repeat rules beyond the subset: "the last Friday of the month", "the second Tuesday", several days of the month, or hourly repeats.
- Email (SMTP), SMS, chat apps, or any other reminder channel. Web push is the only one beside the in-app list.
- A default reminder for every dated thought, a per-user "untimed reminders at" hour, snooze, and quiet hours.
- Alarms (`VALARM`) in the calendar feed; the app sends its own reminders.
- Importing a calendar, or two-way sync. The feed is read-only.
- An operator switch to turn the feed off; it is off only in demo mode.
- An operator switch to turn the job runner off. Only tests turn it off, with a module flag (decision 2).
- More than one backend process, and moving the demo cleanup loop into the runner. Both can come later.
- Showing a repeat in the library rows or the sources list. Their answers carry `when.repeat`, since `when_of()` writes it, but the rows do not show it.
- Reminders in list answers (Upcoming items, library rows, search sources, `target`). Only the one-thought answers and the export carry `when.reminders` (decision 16).
- The weekly review and resurfacing jobs themselves (feature 7).

## Decisions

### 1. One job runner, in the backend process, with its schedule in Postgres

`app/jobs/runner.py` holds a `Job` (`name`, `every: timedelta`, `run: async (JobContext) -> None`, `timeout`) and one loop task. `app/jobs/__init__.py` builds the list from the settings (`build_jobs(settings)`): this change registers `reminders` (every 30 seconds) and `prune-notifications` (every 24 hours), both added with the reminders (task group 3). Feature 7 adds its own entries to this list.

A new table `scheduled_jobs` keeps one row per job: `name TEXT PRIMARY KEY`, `next_run_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`, `running_since TIMESTAMPTZ NULL`. Nothing else is stored: no endpoint or page reads a last result, and a failure is logged (decision 2). At start the runner inserts a row for each job that has none, with `next_run_at` now. A row whose job is no longer registered is left alone.

The loop, while not stopping:

1. `now = utc_now()` (the clock from `app/thoughts/dates.py`, so tests fix it).
2. For each job, in list order, claim it: `UPDATE scheduled_jobs SET next_run_at = :now + every, running_since = :now WHERE name = :name AND next_run_at <= :now`. One row changed means this loop owns the run. Run it under `asyncio.timeout(job.timeout)`, with a `JobContext(sessionmaker, now)`. Then set `running_since = NULL`.
3. Sleep until the earliest `next_run_at` of the registered jobs' rows (`WHERE name IN (<registered names>)`), at least 1 second and at most 30 seconds, or until `stop()` sets the stop event. With no registered jobs, or no such time, sleep 30 seconds. A row left over from a job that is no longer registered never counts: its old `next_run_at` would otherwise make every sleep 0 and the loop spin.

Each pass of steps 1 to 3 runs inside a `try`/`except`, so an error in the loop's own database work never ends the loop (decision 2, Loop error).

Jobs run one after another in the one task. A job opens its own sessions from the context, as the turn does. `run_due(now)` does steps 1 and 2 once; tests call it directly instead of starting the loop.

The runner gives each due run at least once, never two at the same time. A job that must not repeat its effect makes its writes idempotent itself (decision 9). The claim also keeps a second process, started by mistake, from running the same job at the same moment, as `TURN_CLAIM_TIMEOUT` guards turns.

**Alternatives:** sleep until the earliest `next_run_at` of every row (rejected: a leftover row of a job no longer registered is never claimed, so its past time would make the loop spin, and an empty table has no earliest time); APScheduler (rejected: a dependency with its own job store and its own idea of misfires, for two interval jobs); `last_finished_at` and `last_error` columns (rejected: nothing reads them, and the log already holds the failure with its traceback); a worker container or host cron (rejected: a second service, which this project avoids); `pg_cron` (rejected: an extension not every Postgres has, and the work is Python); one ad hoc loop per feature, like the demo cleanup (rejected: each would redo its own shutdown, catch-up, and error handling, and none would survive a restart's timing).

### 2. Catch-up, failures, and shutdown in the runner

- **Catch-up.** A job whose `next_run_at` passed during downtime runs once at start, not once per missed interval: the claim sets the next run from now, not from the missed time. What a job missed is the job's business (decision 9 caps reminders).
- **Failure.** An exception or a timeout is logged with the job name (`logger.exception`, message `job failed: <name>`), and the job is next due at `now + min(every, 5 minutes)`. The loop goes on with the next job. A failing job never stops the others, and never loops hot.
- **Loop error.** The loop's own database work, outside any `job.run` (the start-up inserts and resets, the claim `UPDATE`, clearing `running_since`, the backoff write, and reading the earliest `next_run_at` for the sleep), can raise too, for example an `OperationalError` while the Postgres container restarts. A task made with `asyncio.create_task` that raises ends quietly, and no job would run again until the backend restarts. So each pass of the loop, the start-up step included, is wrapped in `try`/`except Exception`: the error is logged with its traceback (`logger.exception`, message `job runner error`), the loop sleeps 30 seconds (or until `stop()`), and goes on. `asyncio.CancelledError` is not caught. The loop ends only through `stop()`. A claim that committed before the error leaves `running_since` set; the job then waits for its `next_run_at`, as after a failure, and the next start treats the row as a crash.
- **Crash.** A row with `running_since` set at start belongs to a run that never finished. The start sets its `next_run_at` to now, so it runs again at once. With one process, as for turn claims, every such row is from a stopped process.
- **Shutdown.** The lifespan exit stops the runner and the running turns together: `results = await asyncio.gather(runner.stop(TURN_SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT), running.shutdown(TURN_SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT), return_exceptions=True)`, logs each exception in `results` (`logger.error` with its traceback, message `shutdown step failed`), then cancels the cleanup loop and disposes the engine as now. With `return_exceptions=True`, an error from one side never cuts the other short: without it, `gather` would raise at the first error and the lifespan would dispose the engine while the turns' shielded `answer_interrupted` stores were still running. `runner.stop()` sets the stop event, waits up to 5 seconds for a running job to finish, then cancels it. A cancelled job's open transaction rolls back. `runner.stop()` writes nothing after the cancel: the job's row keeps `running_since` from its committed claim, and the next start sets its `next_run_at` to now (Crash, above), so it runs again at once, as the spec asks. So the exit makes no database write after the 5-second wait, just before the engine is disposed. `runner.stop()` then shuts down the push executor without waiting for it (decision 11), so a push send still in a thread does not hold up the lifespan exit or the event loop's close. The two waits overlap, so the exit still takes about 5 seconds, as `background-turns` bounds it, and the turns' shielded `answer_interrupted` stores finish well inside Docker's 10-second grace.
- **Start.** The lifespan starts the runner after `release_stale_turns` and `open_setup`, always, in every auth mode: demo users get in-app reminders too. There is no operator setting to turn it off. Tests need one, so `app/jobs/__init__.py` holds a module flag, `JOBS_ENABLED = True`, that the lifespan reads and only tests change: an autouse fixture in `tests/conftest.py` sets it to `False` for every test, and the runner's own tests set it back, so the real reminder and prune jobs never run inside a TestClient lifespan on SQLite. It is not read from the environment, and `.env.example` and `docs/setup.md` do not list it.

**Alternatives:** retry a failed job at once (rejected: a job that fails on every run would loop); let a loop error end the task and restart it from a done-callback (rejected: a second path to start, bound, and stop, for what a `try` around each pass gives); keep `next_run_at` as claimed after a cancel with `running_since` cleared (rejected: a weekly job stopped by a deploy would wait a week); set `next_run_at` back to the claim time under `running.shielded()` after the cancel (rejected: it repeats what the start-up already does for a row with `running_since` set, and adds a write after the 5-second wait while the engine is about to be disposed); a bare `gather` without `return_exceptions` (rejected: an error from `runner.stop()` would cut the turns' interrupted-answer stores short); a `JOBS_ENABLED` setting for operators (rejected: with it off, the card and the sheet still offer reminders that never fire, and nothing in the app says so; the feature asks for no switch, and only tests need one); stop the runner first, then the turns (rejected: up to 5 plus 5 seconds, plus the engine, reaches Docker's 10-second SIGKILL and can cut off the turns' interrupted-answer stores); a shorter bound for the runner alone (rejected: the overlap gives the job the same 5 seconds at no cost to the turns).

### 3. The repeat is a small shape of our own inside `when`

```
"when": {
  "start": "2026-11-09", "end": null, "time_zone": "Europe/Oslo",
  "repeat": {"every": "week", "interval": 1, "weekdays": ["mon"], "until": null},
  "reminders": [0, 1440]
}
```

- `every`: `day`, `week`, `month`, or `year`.
- `interval`: a whole number from 1 to 99, default 1. JSON `true`, `false`, `1.5`, `60.0`, and `"2"` are not whole numbers: `bool` is a subclass of `int` in Python, so the check refuses `bool`, `float`, and `str` before it tests the range. The reminder offsets are checked the same way. "Every other week" is `week` with 2.
- `weekdays`: only with `week`: a list of `mon` … `sun`. The start's weekday is always added; a missing or empty list means the start's weekday only.
- A `month` repeat falls on the start's day of the month, a `year` repeat on the start's month and day. A month without that day is skipped, as RFC 5545 and calendar apps do: a repeat from 31 January skips February.
- `until`: an optional last day, `YYYY-MM-DD`, not before the start day. An occurrence on that day still counts.
- A repeating date's end, when it has one, must be on its start day ("10:00 to 11:00 every Tuesday"). So an occurrence never spans days, and two occurrences never overlap.
- The start is the first occurrence.

`parse_when()` checks `repeat` with the rest and refuses it naming `when` (`when: the repeat …`). `repeat: null` or a missing key means no repeat. A `when_choices` choice takes the same fields as `when`, apart from the zone and the repeat: a repeating date never gets two choices (decision 7), so the choice schema has no `repeat`, and `parse_proposal()` drops a `repeat` the model puts on a choice anyway and keeps the choice.

**Alternatives:** an RRULE string on the wire (rejected: the model writes bad RRULEs, and the card and the sheet would have to parse one); a `repeat` on each choice (rejected: the spec says a repeating date gets no choices, and two rules about the same field would disagree); the full RRULE (rejected: `BYSETPOS` and `BYDAY=-1FR` need a much harder form, for rare cases; listed as a non-goal); a weekday bit mask on the wire (rejected: unreadable for the model).

### 4. Four columns for the repeat

`thoughts` gains `repeat_every TEXT NULL` (check constraint `repeat_every IN ('day','week','month','year')`, named `repeat_every` so the convention makes it `ck_thoughts_repeat_every`, added in batch mode as `thought-status` does), `repeat_interval SMALLINT NULL`, `repeat_weekdays TEXT NULL` (canonical `mon,wed`, Monday first), and `repeat_until DATE NULL`. All are null when the thought does not repeat. `DateColumns` gains the four fields, so `parse_when()` and `when_of()` stay the one pair of converters, and `values()` still holds only columns, so it can still be splatted into `Thought(...)` and `setattr`. `parse_when()` also returns the checked reminder offsets, in a `reminders: tuple[int, ...]` field of `DateColumns` that `values()` leaves out; the store hands it to `sync_reminders()` (decision 8). No new index: the existing `(user_id, starts_on)` index still narrows by start, and a user's repeating thoughts are few.

`when_of()` reads the four columns whenever it reads the date, so every place that selects only some columns and then calls `when_of()` or `thought_date_text()` selects the four too:

- `list_upcoming()`'s select in `app/thoughts/store.py`, which also gains `Thought.created_at` beside the `Thought.id` it already selects: today it orders by both only in SQL, and the merge of decision 5 sorts in Python by the shown occurrence, then `created_at`, then `id`, so both of its queries select them.
- `conversation_refs()`'s select in `app/chat/refs.py`, with the four fields added to `SeenThought` and `date_fields()`.
- `thought-library`'s `list_thoughts()` `load_only`, where a column left out is a `MissingGreenlet` 500 for every repeating thought.
- the `target` lookup of proposal parts (decision 6).

Places that load whole `Thought` rows (`get_thought()`, the search, the export's batch query, the similar-thought candidates) need nothing. `ThoughtOut.when` and `UpcomingItem.when` are widened from `dict[str, str | None]` to `dict[str, Any] | None`, since `repeat` is an object and `reminders` a list; `when_of()` stays their one writer.

**Alternatives:** one JSON column (rejected, as `thought-dates` decision 1 rejected it: the Upcoming and search queries need `repeat_every IS NOT NULL` and `repeat_until`); a separate `thought_repeats` table (rejected: one-to-one data with a join on every date read); a pydantic `WhenOut` model for the answers (rejected: `reminders` is left out of list answers, decision 16, and an optional key in a model needs `exclude_unset` on every route; `when_of()` is already the one writer and its tests pin the shape); reminders in `values()` (rejected: `Thought(...)` and `setattr` cannot take them).

### 5. Expanding a repeat: `dateutil.rrule`, one entry per thought

`app/thoughts/repeats.py` turns the columns into a `dateutil.rrule.rrule` (`DAILY`/`WEEKLY`/`MONTHLY`/`YEARLY`, `interval`, `byweekday` for weeks, `bymonthday` for months, `bymonth` and `bymonthday` for years, `wkst=MO`, `until`, `dtstart` the start day) and answers two questions: the first occurrence that ends on or after a day (`next_on_or_after`), and the first occurrence inside a range of days (`first_between(from, to)`). Either bound of `first_between` can be `None`, as `dated_from` and `dated_to` each can: `first_between(None, to)` is the start when it is not after `to`, else none; `first_between(from, None)` is `next_on_or_after(from)`. It also writes the repeat as text: `every week on Mon`, `every 2 weeks on Mon, Thu`, `every month on day 15`, `every year on 3 Nov`, `every day`, with `until 2026-12-31` when it has a last day.

A repeating thought shows as **one entry**, at one occurrence, never as one entry per occurrence:

- **Upcoming.** `list_upcoming()` runs its query for thoughts that do not repeat (as now, plus `repeat_every IS NULL`, still `LIMIT 201`), and a second query for the user's open repeating thoughts whose `repeat_until` is null or not before `from`, at most 1,000, by start, with no cut before the merge. The second query has the same `Thought.user_id == user.id` and `status = 'open'` filters as the first, written once in a shared `where` list so neither can drop one. Both select the same columns, `Thought.created_at` and `Thought.id` included (decision 4). For each repeating thought, it takes `next_on_or_after(from)`, and drops one with none. It merges both lists in date order by the shown occurrence (its start day, a day with no time first, then its start time), then `created_at`, then `id`, and cuts at 200. `more` is true when the merged list holds more than 200. The SQL limit of the first query cannot lose an item the merged list would keep: the first 200 of the merge hold at most 200 one-offs, and those are the first 200 one-offs in date order. Each item gains `occurs: {start, end}`, the occurrence shown; for a thought that does not repeat it equals the stored start and end. The page groups by `occurs.start` and shows the repeat text under the title. A thought that does not repeat and started before today still goes under "Today", as `thought-dates` groups it; a repeating thought goes under its next occurrence's day, even when its stored start is before today, so the `upcoming-thoughts` delta also modifies "The Upcoming page groups by day" (decision 15).
- **Search by the thought's date.** Before the main query, `search_thoughts()` loads the user's repeating thoughts (owner filter `Thought.user_id == user.id`, and the call's status and category filters) that could fall in the range (`starts_on <= :dated_to` when `dated_to` is given, `repeat_until` null or not before `:dated_from` when `dated_from` is given) and keeps the ids with a `first_between()` occurrence. The date filter becomes `(repeat_every IS NULL AND <overlap test>) OR id IN (:ids)`. With a query, ranking works as now inside that filter. With no query, `list_dated()` keeps its SQL date order and `LIMIT` for the thoughts that do not repeat only (`repeat_every IS NULL AND <overlap test>`, with the other filters), loads the repeating ids with the same owner filter and the same other filters (status, category, tags, filed days), built from the one filter list the one-off select uses so the two cannot drift apart, merges the two in Python by each thought's shown occurrence (`first_between()`, a day with no time first, then the time, then filing time), and cuts at the limit. The count that matched is the one-off count plus the repeating ones that passed. So repeating thoughts with old starts never fill the SQL limit ahead of later one-offs. `format_search_result()` prints the shown occurrence after `on`, then `· repeats every week on Mon`.
- **What the model sees elsewhere.** The seen-thoughts note and both candidate lists write each thought with `thought_entry()` (Context), which prints `· on <date>` and, from `thought-status`, the status marker. For a repeating thought, `thought_entry()` adds `· repeats every week on Mon` after the date, as `thought-status` added its marker: `T3 · Take the bins out · filed 2026-10-01 · on Mon 2026-10-05 · repeats every week on Mon`. The date stays the stored start, the first occurrence, as everywhere the model reads it; the repeat text says it goes on. Without it, a seen `Take the bins out` reads as a one-off task dated in the past, and the model would mark it done on "I took the bins out" (decision 7), most of all in the `/push` candidate round, where no search runs first. `SeenThought` carries the four columns (decision 4); the candidates are whole `Thought` rows.

`dateutil` is pure Python and also comes with `icalendar` (decision 13), so the rule the app expands is the same rule a calendar app reads from the feed.

**Alternatives:** expand into one entry per occurrence (rejected: "every day" would fill the 200-item page and the search result, and spend the model's tokens on copies); one SQL select for both kinds, sorted in Python after its `LIMIT` (rejected: repeating thoughts with old starts sort first in SQL and push later one-offs past the limit before Python sees them); the next occurrence instead of the start in `thought_entry()` (rejected: the note would need today's date and the series' end, and the search result already shows the first occurrence when no date filter is given; the repeat text is what the model needs); our own expansion code (rejected: month-end and leap-day rules would differ from what calendar apps do with the same feed); materialise occurrences in a table (rejected: a table to keep in step on every edit, for a few rows per user).

### 6. Done ends the series

Status stays one column per thought. Marking a repeating thought done (from a status card or the sheet's toggle) works as for any thought: it leaves the Upcoming page, its reminders stop (decision 8), and it leaves the feed. Setting it back to open brings it all back from the next occurrence.

So one occurrence is never marked done. The prompt says so (decision 7), and the status card for a repeating thought adds a line, "Marking it done stops it repeating." `thought-status`' card and confirm otherwise do not change.

`target` carries no date today (Context), so the backend adds one field. The `target` lookup (the one query that serves the stream event, the offered proposal, and the conversation's messages answer, which `thought-status` extends to mark ids) also reads the four repeat columns and puts `repeat` on `target`: the `when.repeat` shape, or null for a thought that does not repeat. It keeps the lookup's `Thought.user_id == user.id` filter, so a mark with another user's id still gets `{deleted: true}` and no `repeat`. The card shows the line when `target.repeat` is not null.

**Alternatives:** a table of done occurrences (rejected: a per-occurrence state on the Upcoming page, the feed, and reminders, for a brain-dump app whose repeating items are mostly reminders to do something); Done moves the start to the next occurrence and keeps the thought open, as some to-do apps do (rejected: it silently rewrites the stored date, a reopen cannot undo it, and `thought-status`' rule that a status change touches nothing else would break); the whole `when` on `target` (rejected: the card asks one question and needs only whether it repeats; reminders would need the second query of decision 16 in every payload).

### 7. The model, the card, and the sheet

**Tool schema and prompt.** `DATE_SCHEMA` gains `repeat` and `reminders`, both optional. The `when` description adds:

- Give `repeat` only when the user says the thing repeats ("every Monday", "each month on the 1st", "every other week", "daily"). Then the start is the first occurrence, today or later, and today counts: "every Friday" said on a Friday starts today. A repeat never gets two choices. These are exceptions to `thought-dates`' weekday rules (a weekday is the soonest such day after today, and today's weekday gets two choices), so the `conversation-agent` delta modifies "The tools carry dates" instead of adding a rule beside it (decision 15).
- Give `reminders` only when the user asks to be reminded: minutes before the start, `0` for "at the time". "Remind me" with no time gives `[0]`. A day with no time is reminded at 09:00, so "remind me the day before" is `[1440]`.

The status lines of the prompt add: a repeating thought, shown with `repeats …` in the seen-thoughts note, the candidate lists, and the search result (decision 5), is not marked done because one occurrence happened; mark it done only when the user says it has ended or they stopped it. This one line is the exception to each of `thought-status`' mark rules, which the `conversation-agent` delta modifies (decision 15):

- A seen thought, and a thought a search finds: no mark for "I took the bins out".
- A similar-thought candidate, including under `/push`: no mark. The model's next proposal holds a new note about what the user did, or the model proposes nothing more and replies. A part that has already gone through the candidate round needs no new rule for either.
- The candidate round: `thought-status`' repeated-task line (`A repeated task is usually a new task, unless the user says they finished it.`) is followed, when a listed candidate repeats, by `A thought marked "repeats" is marked done only when the user says it has ended or they stopped it.`, written by the same candidate-round formatter.
- The held-proposal note: `thought-status`' sentence about a held part the user finished gains `If that part repeats, doing it once does not end it: keep the part, with its repeat, and do not say it can be passed over.` So `/push take the bins out every Monday`, held, then "I took the bins out" keeps the bins card.
- A finished thing beside a held proposal: `thought-status`' line that the model makes one proposal holding the held parts and the mark gains `When the finished thing repeats, keep the held parts and add no mark, unless the user says it has ended or they stopped it.` So an oat-milk proposal held, then "I took the bins out" with a seen weekly `Take the bins out`, keeps the oat-milk part and adds no mark.
- Several open matches: `thought-status`' line that only the newest match is marked gains `A match marked "repeats" is never the one marked for one occurrence.`

**Parsing.** `parse_proposal()` checks `repeat` and `reminders` with the date. A `repeat` on a `when_choices` choice is dropped and the choice kept, as the choice schema has no `repeat` (decision 3). A bad `repeat` is dropped and the date is kept; bad `reminders` are dropped and the rest is kept (spec: "Not valid from the model"), as a bad `when` is dropped and the part kept. `held_proposal_note()`'s `Date:` line adds `, repeats every week on Mon` and `, reminders 0 and 1440 minutes before`.

**Confirm and `PATCH`.** `when` stays one value: the object replaces the date, its repeat, and its reminders together. A missing `repeat` key is no repeat, and a missing `reminders` key is no reminders. Merge keeps the first part's `when` whole.

**Fields.** `date-fields.tsx` gains two small parts, used by the card and the sheet alike:

- `repeat-fields.tsx`: a select (Does not repeat, Every day, Every week, Every month, Every year), an "every N" number, weekday toggles for weeks (the start's weekday on and locked), and an optional "Until" date. A repeat with an end on another day is not offered: choosing a repeat moves the end to the start day, keeping its time. When the moved end would not be after the start (an overnight `2026-11-06T22:00` to `2026-11-07T01:00` would become 22:00 to 01:00 on the same day, which the store refuses; a day range with no times would become one day to the same day), the end is cleared instead. The start never changes.
- `reminder-fields.tsx`: up to five rows, each a select. With a time: At the time, 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes, 1 or 2 hours, 1 or 2 days, 1 week before. With no time: On the day (09:00), 1 day, 2 days, 1 week before. "Add reminder" and a remove button per row. A stored value with no preset shows as "N minutes before".

**The whole `when` on every send.** Since a missing key now clears, every `when` the card and the sheet send carries `repeat` and `reminders`. `whenOfText(date, timeZone, repeat, reminders)` builds all five keys, and `sameWhen()` compares all five (weekdays and reminders as sorted lists). So moving only the start of a weekly thought with reminders sends `when` with the same repeat and reminders, and opening the sheet and saving with no change sends no `when` at all.

**The zone on a sheet save.** `thought-dates` decision 13 sends `when` only when the start or end changed, with the browser's zone. Now `when` is also sent when the repeat or the reminders changed. When only those changed, the sheet sends the stored `time_zone`, not the browser's, so adding a reminder from another zone does not move the times.

**Alternatives:** `repeat` and `reminders` as part fields beside `when` (rejected: the request names `when.repeat`, clearing the date must clear both, and one value keeps `PATCH`'s missing-key rules simple); a separate reminders endpoint (rejected: a second way to change a thought's date data, and the card would need two writes); a missing key in a `PATCH` `when` keeps the stored repeat or reminders (rejected: `when: {start}` would then mean "move it" for some keys and "clear it" for others, and an old client could never clear them; the frontend sends all keys instead).

### 8. Reminders are rows with their next fire time

`thought_reminders`: `id`, `user_id` (cascade), `thought_id` (cascade), `offset_minutes INTEGER NOT NULL` (0 to 40,320, four weeks), `next_fire_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL`. Unique `(thought_id, offset_minutes)`; index on `next_fire_at` where it is not null (Postgres only, declared like the date index). At most five per thought, counted after `parse_when()` merges duplicates: `[0, 5, 10, 15, 30, 30]` is five. The occurrence a fire time belongs to is not stored: it is `next_fire_at` plus the offset, read in the thought's zone, and the job works it out from the thought on every run (decision 9).

`sync_reminders(session, thought, offsets, now)` in `app/reminders.py` brings a thought's rows in line with its `when` and status, in place, keyed by `(thought_id, offset_minutes)`: a row whose offset is no longer asked for is deleted, a new offset gets a row, and a kept offset keeps its row and id. For each row, `next_fire_at` is the fire time of the earliest occurrence whose fire time is after `now`, with one exception: a row whose stored `next_fire_at` is already past, at most 24 hours ago, and still a fire time of this reminder under the new `when` (its occurrence is still an occurrence) keeps it, because the job has not sent it yet. The fire time is the occurrence's start in the thought's zone (`ZoneInfo(time_zone)`), at 09:00 for a day with no time, minus the offset, as UTC. A done thought, a thought with no date, and a series that has ended get `next_fire_at = NULL`. A fire time already past when the reminder is first set is skipped: "1 day before" set on a dinner tonight sends nothing for tonight.

So a reminder due at 18:00:00, which the job picks up within about a minute, is not lost when the user adds a second reminder at 18:00:10: the 18:00 row keeps its fire time, and the job sends it. A date that moved drops the old fire time, since it is no longer a fire time of the new date.

It is called in the same transaction as every write that can change the answer, inside the non-committing cores, so no caller can skip it: `add_thought()` (confirm calls it directly, and `store_thought()` through it), `apply_update()` when `when` is not `_UNSET` (`PATCH` through `update_thought()`), and `set_status()`. A delete cascades the rows.

**Two saves at once.** `sync_reminders()` first takes `SELECT thoughts.id ... FOR UPDATE` on the thought's row (Postgres; SQLite ignores it, and lets one writer in at a time anyway). So two `PATCH`es of the same thought, sent at once from two tabs, run their syncs one after the other: the second reads the rows the first committed, and neither inserts an offset the other already inserted, which would fail on `UNIQUE (thought_id, offset_minutes)` with a 500. The lock is on the thought, which the reminder job also locks with `SKIP LOCKED` (decision 9), so a save waits for at most one batch's commit and the job skips a thought being saved; neither waits on the other's later rows, so they cannot deadlock.

**The "changed" date.** `thought-editing` requires every saved change to show a new "changed" date in the sheet. A `when` that differs from the stored one only in `reminders` changes no column of `thoughts`, so SQLAlchemy emits no `UPDATE` for the thought and its `onupdate=func.now()` never fires. So `sync_reminders()` returns whether the set of offsets changed, and when it did, `apply_update()` sets `thought.updated_at = func.now()` itself. A change of `next_fire_at` alone (a status change, a sync that only moves fire times) does not count: `set_status()` keeps `updated_at` as `thought-status` requires. Two effects, as after a date edit: `thought-appending`'s undo of the last addition refuses with 409 `thought_changed` once a reminder was added or removed after it, since the thought's `updated_at` no longer equals the addition's `after_updated_at`; and an addition proposed before the reminder change gets 409 on confirm, since its `based_on` no longer matches.

A local time that does not exist (a spring-forward gap) resolves with `fold=0`, as `zoneinfo` does: 02:30 on that day fires at 03:30.

**Alternatives:** scan every dated thought on each tick (rejected: work grows with all users' thoughts, and "already sent" would need its own record); reminders as JSON on the thought (rejected: no index on the next fire time); a fixed 08:00 or a per-user hour for untimed days (09:00 chosen; per-user is a non-goal); delete and rewrite all rows from `now` on each sync (rejected: a reminder due but not yet sent would be skipped by any save in the minute after it came due); store the occurrence on the row (rejected: a copy of what the fire time and the thought already give, which every sync would have to keep in step, for no reader); call it from `store_thought()` and `update_thought()` (rejected: confirm calls `add_thought()` directly, so confirmed reminders would never get a fire time); insert new offsets with `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (thought_id, offset_minutes) DO NOTHING` and read the rows again, with no lock (rejected: it ends the 500, but two saves with different reminders, each deleting what it does not ask for and inserting what it does, could leave the rows of both, which neither `when` holds); leave `updated_at` alone on a reminder-only change (rejected: the sheet would show the old "changed" date after a save, which `thought-editing` does not allow).

### 9. The reminder job delivers once and catches up with a cap

Every 30 seconds, the `reminders` job (timeout 90 seconds) runs batches until nothing is due, then pushes:

1. In one transaction, select up to 200 rows with `next_fire_at <= now`, `ORDER BY next_fire_at`, joined to their thought and to the thought's user, with `FOR UPDATE OF thought_reminders, thoughts SKIP LOCKED FOR KEY SHARE OF users SKIP LOCKED` (left out on SQLite). SQLAlchemy's `with_for_update()` writes only one locking clause, so on Postgres the query adds both clauses itself, at the end of the statement (`suffix_with()`). Every row the batch will touch, and every row its notification insert's foreign keys check, is then locked before the batch writes anything, and a row someone else holds is skipped instead of waited on:
   - A thought that a user is deleting, or whose content "Delete content" is removing, is skipped: the delete holds the thought row.
   - A user whose account "Delete account" (`delete_user_data()`) or the demo cleanup (`delete_expired_demo_users()`, which runs in demo mode beside the runner) is deleting is skipped: `DELETE FROM users` holds the user row first, which conflicts with `FOR KEY SHARE`.
   - When the batch locks first, the delete waits for the batch's commit, a fraction of a second, and then deletes the new notifications with everything else.

   Without the `users` clause, the job would hold the reminder and thought rows and then, on its notification insert, wait for a key-share lock on the user row for the foreign key, while the account delete holds the user row and its cascade waits on the job's reminder and thought rows: a deadlock that Postgres ends by aborting one side, which can be the user's "Delete account" (a 500) or the demo cleanup. Without the `thoughts` clause, the same cycle forms with a thought delete. A `PATCH` or status change on a thought in the batch waits for the batch's commit, and a write that only checks a foreign key to the user (a new message, a usage record) does not wait at all, since key-share locks do not conflict with each other.
2. For each row, find the **latest** occurrence whose fire time is at or before `now` (after downtime this can be later than the stored one). When `now` minus that fire time is at most 24 hours, insert a notification (decision 10) with `created_at = ctx.now`, the key `reminder:<thought id>:<offset minutes>:<occurrence start>`, and `ON CONFLICT (user_id, dedupe_key) DO NOTHING`. Older than 24 hours, insert nothing. The key names the thought, the offset, and the occurrence, never the row id, so a row that `sync_reminders()` deleted and made again cannot send the same reminder twice.
3. Move the row on: the next occurrence whose fire time is after `now`, or null.
4. Commit, and keep the notifications this batch actually inserted for the push step.

So one reminder sends at most one notification per run, a backlog of daily occurrences becomes one notification, and a reminder late by more than a day is dropped. A batch is capped at 200, and a run at 10 batches (`REMINDER_BATCH` and `MAX_BATCHES`, module constants tests lower); the next run, 30 seconds later, goes on where this one stopped, since the rows not reached keep their past `next_fire_at`. The database work of a run is short: 10 batches of inserts and updates.

The notification and the move commit together. A crash before the commit redoes the batch; the unique key makes a redo after the commit insert nothing.

**The push step.** After the last batch, the job hands the run's inserted notifications to the push sender (decision 11), with its own budget:

- The cap counts notifications, not sends. At most 5 of a user's notifications per run are pushed, each to every one of the user's subscriptions. A user with more gets one more push to each subscription, "and N more reminders", linking to `/app/notifications`; the in-app list keeps them all. So a user with 10 browsers gets the same 6 pushes on each, 60 sends.
- Users take turns by notification. The send queue is built in rounds: round 1 holds every user's first notification, one send per subscription of that user; round 2 every user's second; up to round 5; then the "and N more" pushes. So one user's volume cannot use up the budget ahead of another user's one reminder, and a user's notification goes to all their browsers before anyone's next one.
- Up to 10 sends at a time, each with a 3-second timeout (`timeout=3`, which `requests` applies to the connect and to each read), on the push module's own `ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=10)` (decision 11).
- 20 seconds in all (`PUSH_BUDGET`, a module constant tests lower). When it runs out, the sends not started are dropped, are never tried again, and are logged as a count (`push budget spent, <n> pushes dropped`); their notifications stay in the in-app list. That is not a job failure. The step also stops starting sends as soon as `runner.stop()` is called (the stop event is on the `JobContext`).
- Pushes go only to users the email allow-list still lets in (decision 17).

The job's 90-second timeout covers the batches and the 20-second push step with room to spare, so an unreachable push service never makes the job time out, and never delays the next run's batches. Push is sent only after the commits, so a crash between a commit and the push loses that push, never doubles it; the in-app notification is there either way.

**Alternatives:** send every missed occurrence (rejected: a week down would send seven "bins" reminders); no cap on lateness (rejected: "dinner at 19:00" three days late is noise); a separate `sent_reminders` table (rejected: the notification row with its unique key is that record); push before the commit (rejected: a crash after the push would push again); push after each batch, one send at a time, inside the 60-second job timeout (rejected: with a push service down, seven 10-second sends time the job out on every run, the cancelled sends count as failures, and the next batches wait); a separate in-process push queue drained by its own task (rejected: one more task to start, stop, and bound at shutdown, for what a budget inside the job gives); a per-user limit on rows per batch (rejected: the database work of 2,000 rows is quick, so one user's rows delay no one's notification; only the pushes are slow, and the per-user cap and turns cover them); a cap of 5 sends per user in total (rejected: a user with 10 browsers would get pushes on only some of them); per user per run, one push for one notification, else a single "N reminders" push (rejected: simpler, but a user with two reminders at 18:00, the common case beyond one, would see no title at all; 5 titles then a count keeps the common cases readable, and the rounds keep the fairness); the row id in the dedupe key (rejected: a rewritten row would get a new id and send again); `FOR UPDATE OF thought_reminders` alone, or with `thoughts` and without `users` (rejected: the deadlocks above); have `delete_user_data()` and the demo cleanup delete the user's thoughts before the user row (rejected: the order would then be a rule every present and future delete path must keep, where the lock clause sits in the one query that takes the locks; and it would not cover a later kind of notification with no thought); `FOR UPDATE OF users` (rejected: it would also block every write that checks the user's foreign key, such as a new message, for the length of the batch).

### 10. Notifications: one table, a list page, and a polled count

`notifications`: `id`, `user_id` (cascade), `thought_id` (cascade, nullable for later kinds), `kind TEXT` (`reminder`; feature 7 adds its own kinds), `title TEXT` (the thought's title when it fired), `body TEXT` (the occurrence only, for example `Fri 2 Oct, 19:00`, or `Mon 9 Nov` for a day with no time, written by the server in English, as `lib/dates.ts` writes dates), `dedupe_key TEXT`, `created_at`, `read_at NULL`. Unique `(user_id, dedupe_key)`; index `(user_id, created_at)`. Deleting a thought deletes its notifications, so a title never outlives "Delete content".

The body has no relative part ("in 1 hour", "today"). It is written once, when the reminder fires, and kept for 30 days: a reminder sent late after downtime (up to 24 hours, decision 9) would say "in 1 hour" about something that has started, and an item read the next day would say "today" about another day. The absolute day and time stay true.

`created_at` is set by the writer, never by a database default: the reminder job sets `created_at = ctx.now`, the run's `utc_now()`. Other models use `server_default=func.now()`, the database's clock, in whole seconds on SQLite; with that, a notification made in a test with a fixed clock would carry the real time, its own `created_at` posted back as `up_to` would be refused as in the future, and the prune test could not place a row 31 days back. The model has no default, so a writer that forgets the time fails on `NOT NULL`. `up_to` and the prune compare against the same clock: the future check against `utc_now()`, and the prune deletes `created_at < ctx.now - 30 days`.

API, all owner-scoped:

- `GET /api/notifications?limit=50` → `{items: [{id, kind, title, body, thought_id, created_at, read_at}], unread}`, newest first, `limit` 1 to 100. `created_at` is served as ISO 8601 with microseconds and `Z`, so the string posted back as `up_to` names the same instant.
- `POST /api/notifications/read` with `{up_to: <created_at>}` → `{unread}`. It marks read every one of the caller's unread notifications created at or before that time, however many there are, and nothing of another user's. A time after `utc_now()` is refused (422 `validation_error` naming `up_to`), so a crafted call cannot mark a notification that has not come yet. `up_to` is typed pydantic `AwareDatetime`, so a time with no offset (`2026-11-05T18:00:10`) or a string that is not a time (`yesterday`) gets the same 422 naming `up_to`, before any comparison: a naive time compared with the aware `utc_now()` would raise `TypeError`, a 500.

The sidebar gets "Notifications" under "Upcoming", with the unread count as a badge and in its accessible name ("Notifications, 3 unread"). It reads the count from `GET /api/notifications?limit=1`, which already answers `unread`; there is no separate count route. It loads the count on mount, every 60 seconds while the page is visible, and on window focus. A failed load (429 `rate_limited`, 401, a network error) keeps the last count shown, shows no error or toast, and is tried again at the next tick or focus. In demo mode, which the sidebar reads from `useDemo()` (`components/demo/demo-context.tsx`, non-null only in demo mode; `app/app/layout.tsx` already fills that context from `demoSettings()` in `lib/demo-settings.ts`, so no new prop is passed to `<Sidebar />`), the count loads on mount, on focus, and on each navigation, never on the 60-second timer: without `TRUSTED_PROXY`, `DEMO_RATE_LIMIT` (60 a minute) is one bucket shared by every visitor, and each idle demo tab polling once a minute would use it up for everyone. A demo reminder still shows within about a minute of the user's next click or return to the tab. `app/app/notifications/page.tsx` lists the newest 50; opening the page sends one `POST` with `up_to` set to the newest listed item's `created_at`, which marks those and every older unread one read, so the count is 0 even when more than 50 were unread. A notification that came after the list was loaded stays unread. The listed ones show as read the next time. An item opens its thought in the side sheet over the page, as the Upcoming page does.

The `prune-notifications` job deletes notifications older than 30 days (`created_at < ctx.now - 30 days`), once a day.

**Alternatives:** poll every 60 seconds in demo mode too (rejected: idle demo tabs would spend the instance's shared request limit, above); show an error when the count cannot load (rejected: a number that is a minute stale is harmless, and a toast every minute while the backend restarts is noise); a separate `GET /api/notifications/unread` for the badge (rejected: the list route already answers `unread`, and a second owner-scoped route is one more query path to test); a relative part in the body ("in 1 hour"), worked out from the send time (rejected: still stale once read later, and the list page can show the age of an item if it ever needs to); `created_at` from `server_default=func.now()` (rejected: a second clock that tests cannot fix, above); `{ids: [...]}` of the listed items (rejected: with more than 50 unread the badge would keep the rest after the page was opened); `{all: true}` (rejected: a notification that arrives between the list load and the `POST` would be marked read unseen); a toast or the browser `Notification` API while the tab is open (rejected: a second channel to test; push covers the closed tab when the operator turns it on); server-sent events for the count (rejected: one more long-lived connection per tab for a number that can be a minute late); the occurrence as structured data the browser formats (rejected: push needs the same text from the server anyway).

### 11. Web push: VAPID keys from the environment, subscriptions tied to the key and to one user, known push services only

**Settings.** `WEB_PUSH_VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `WEB_PUSH_VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` (base64url, as `python -m app.notifications.push keys` prints them), and `WEB_PUSH_SUBJECT` (a `mailto:` or `https:` address, required by VAPID, default `mailto:admin@localhost`). Push is on only when both keys are set. One key without the other stops the backend at start with a message naming the missing one. Demo mode refuses either key, as it refuses `MCP_ENABLED=true`. The setup script does not generate them; `docs/notifications.md` shows the command.

**The offline install.** With keys set, the reminder job sends requests to the browsers' push services (`fcm.googleapis.com` and the others below), which are outside the install. `self-hosting-setup`'s requirement "No runtime calls to the internet" lists the conditions under which neither server calls out, so this change modifies it to add "web push off (no VAPID keys)" to them (the `offline-install` delta, decision 15). The backend does not refuse push when the offline providers file is used: it has no reliable way to know it runs offline, an operator may run the offline overlay on a host that can reach the push services, and push is already off unless the operator sets the keys. `docs/notifications.md` and `docs/offline.md` say to leave the keys unset in an offline install.

The private key must not leak through those refusals. Pydantic puts `input_value=`, the repr of the whole settings input cut to its head and tail, into a model-validator error (Context), so with the private key as the last input the message would end with its last characters. `Settings.model_config` sets `hide_input_in_errors=True`, which also covers `KEY_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, and `WEB_PUSH_VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` is a `SecretStr`, so `repr(settings)` and the doctor's settings check show it as `**********`. The sender reads it with `get_secret_value()`.

**Subscriptions.** `push_subscriptions`: `id`, `user_id` (cascade), `endpoint TEXT UNIQUE` (the push service's URL, at most 2,048 ASCII characters, so it always fits a btree index key), `p256dh TEXT` and `auth TEXT` (the subscription's keys, base64url as the browser sent them), `key_id TEXT` (the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of the VAPID public key the subscription was posted under), `email` (decision 17), `created_at`. At most 10 per user; an eleventh replaces the oldest. No request ever returns a subscription, and no log line holds its endpoint or keys.

The columns are plain, not encrypted. Every subscription is made with the instance's VAPID public key as its `applicationServerKey`, and FCM, Mozilla's autopush, and Apple's push service refuse a push to it that is not signed with the matching private key. That key is in the environment, next to `KEY_ENCRYPTION_KEY`. So whoever reads the table alone cannot push, and whoever can also read the environment could decrypt an encrypted row anyway: encryption would stop nothing. Plain columns let the endpoint itself be the unique key, with no hash column and no re-encryption when an endpoint moves to another user.

**The key a subscription was made with.** A subscription works only with the key pair it was made under. When the operator changes `WEB_PUSH_VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY` and `WEB_PUSH_VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`, every stored subscription is dead for good: each send is refused by the push service, with an answer that is often neither 404 nor 410. So each row keeps `key_id`, the fingerprint of the public key it was posted under, and the sender deletes, without trying to send, every row whose `key_id` differs from the current key's (Sending, below). The browser holds the same knowledge: `subscription.options.applicationServerKey` is the key it subscribed with. The Settings section and the restore below compare it with `GET /api/notifications/push`'s `public_key`, and treat a browser subscription made under another key as no subscription: the section shows Off, and nothing is posted. "Turn on" then calls `unsubscribe()` on the stale subscription before it subscribes with the new key, since `pushManager.subscribe()` refuses a different key while the old subscription exists. `docs/notifications.md` says new keys mean each user turns browser notifications on again in each browser.

A push endpoint belongs to a browser profile, not to an account: after user A signs out and B signs in on the same browser, `pushManager.subscribe()` hands B the subscription A stored. So:

- `GET /api/notifications/push` → `{enabled, public_key}`. Whether this browser is subscribed is the browser's own `registration.pushManager.getSubscription()`, where the registration comes from `navigator.serviceWorker.getRegistration()`, made under the current `public_key`, and turned on by the signed-in user (the marker, below); the server is not asked. With no registration, or no subscription on it, the browser is not subscribed.
- `POST /api/notifications/push/subscriptions` with the browser's `PushSubscription.toJSON()` → 204. This is "Turn on". It is an upsert by `endpoint`, written as one statement: `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (endpoint) DO UPDATE SET user_id = excluded.user_id, p256dh = excluded.p256dh, auth = excluded.auth, key_id = excluded.key_id, email = excluded.email, created_at = excluded.created_at` (the dialect's `insert().on_conflict_do_update()`, which Postgres and SQLite both have). A new endpoint is stored for the caller; an endpoint stored for the caller is updated; an endpoint stored for another user is moved to the caller by the same statement. Then, in the same transaction, the caller's rows beyond the newest 10 are deleted. The answer is 204 in all three cases, so it never says whether another account had the endpoint, and a browser gets pushes for at most one user, the last one who turned them on there. 404 `not_found` when push is off.
- The same route with `{restore: true}` beside the subscription is the restore, and never moves a row: `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (endpoint) DO UPDATE SET p256dh = excluded.p256dh, auth = excluded.auth, key_id = excluded.key_id, email = excluded.email, created_at = excluded.created_at WHERE push_subscriptions.user_id = excluded.user_id`. It inserts the row when it is missing (the server deleted it after a 404 or 410, or the browser renewed its subscription under a new endpoint) and refreshes the caller's own row; a row another user holds is left as it is. Then the cap of 10, as above. The answer is 204 every time, so it says nothing about another account either. Only "Turn on" moves a row from one user to another: a user who never turned push on in this browser cannot take another account's subscription by opening a page.
- Both are one statement each, never select-then-insert: the restore runs on every app load, in two tabs at once, and twice in a row under React StrictMode in development, and two such calls would both find no row, both insert, and one would fail on `UNIQUE(endpoint)` with a 500. With the one statement, two calls at once both answer 204 and leave one row.
- **The body is checked.** Both routes parse the body with a pydantic model before anything else. `endpoint` is a string of at most 2,048 printable ASCII characters (a browser's endpoint is a URL, and the limit then keeps it inside a btree index key), then `allowed_endpoint()` (below) for the `POST`. `restore` is an optional boolean, default false. `keys.p256dh` is base64url (padding optional) that decodes to 65 bytes starting with `0x04`, an uncompressed P-256 point; `keys.auth` is base64url that decodes to 16 bytes. `expirationTime` is accepted and ignored. Any other key is ignored and never stored. Anything else (no `keys`, an empty `auth`, a `p256dh` of the wrong length, an endpoint of 10,000 characters) is 422 `validation_error` naming the field, and nothing is stored. Only `endpoint`, `keys.p256dh`, and `keys.auth` are stored, with the server's own `key_id`, so a stored row is small and every stored key can encrypt a payload. Without the check, a subscription with no `auth` or a bad key would be stored, every send would fail with an encryption error that is neither 404 nor 410, so it would never be deleted, and it would fail and log on every reminder.
- `DELETE /api/notifications/push/subscriptions` with `{endpoint}` → 204. It deletes only the caller's row with that endpoint. An unknown endpoint, or one stored for another user, answers 204 and is left in place.
- **Who turned it on here.** The browser keeps a `localStorage` marker, `ff:push-user`, with the id of the account (from `GET /api/me`) that turned push on in it. "Turn on" writes it after its `POST`; "Turn off" and `signOut()` remove it; every read and write is wrapped in `try`/`catch`, and a marker that cannot be read counts as none. The Settings section shows "On in this browser" only when the browser has a subscription under the current key and the marker names the signed-in user; otherwise it shows Off, and "Turn on" moves the subscription to this user, as the spec allows the last one who turned it on. The marker is not a credential: it only decides what the section shows and whether a restore is sent, and the restore cannot move a row whatever the marker says.
- **The restore on load.** A browser can renew its subscription on its own, under a new endpoint, and the server deletes a row after a 404 or 410; either way the browser would get no pushes until the user next opened Settings. So `restorePush()` in `lib/push.ts` runs once when the app layout mounts (a small client component, `components/notifications/push-restore.tsx`, in `app/app/layout.tsx`) and when the Settings section loads. It reads the marker first and stops when there is none, so a browser that never turned push on makes no request. Otherwise it gets `GET /api/me` and `GET /api/notifications/push`, and only when push is enabled, the marker names this user, `getRegistration()` gives a registration with a subscription, and its `applicationServerKey` equals `public_key`, it posts that subscription with `restore: true`, once. It never calls `register()`, `subscribe()`, or `navigator.serviceWorker.ready`, and it does nothing in demo mode (`useDemo()` non-null), where push is off and requests share the demo limit. A failure is ignored and tried again at the next load.
- **Sign-out.** `logOut()` in `lib/account.ts` becomes `signOut()`: it removes the `ff:push-user` marker, then gets `navigator.serviceWorker.getRegistration()`, which resolves to undefined when no worker is registered, and with none goes to `/auth/logout` at once. With one (quick-capture's worker, below, may register one on every page, so a registration alone says nothing about push), it gets `registration.pushManager.getSubscription()`, which answers at once from the browser, and when there is one sends the `DELETE` for it and calls `unsubscribe()`, with a 2-second limit and errors ignored, then goes to `/auth/logout`; with none it goes at once. It never awaits `navigator.serviceWorker.ready`, which never settles where no worker was registered: push is off by default, so every sign-out would wait the full 2 seconds. Every sign-out link calls it instead of a plain `<a href="/auth/logout">`: `app/app/layout.tsx`, `app/settings/page.tsx`, `app/setup/page.tsx`, `app/auth/not-allowed/page.tsx`, and "Delete account" after its tick (where the `DELETE` answers 401, ignored, and `unsubscribe()` still runs). So the next person on that browser gets none of the previous user's reminder titles. A browser closed without signing out keeps its subscription; `docs/notifications.md` says to turn browser notifications off before handing over a shared browser profile.

**Allowed endpoints.** The backend sends an HTTP request to whatever endpoint is stored, so a stored `http://db:5432/` would be a server-side request to the operator's network. `allowed_endpoint(url)` parses the endpoint with `urllib.parse.urlsplit` and accepts it only when all of these hold: the scheme is `https`; there is no user info (`@`); there is no port, or port 443; the host name is not an IP literal (`ipaddress.ip_address()` fails on it, brackets removed); and the lowercased host name equals `fcm.googleapis.com`, `updates.push.services.mozilla.com`, or `web.push.apple.com`, or ends with `.push.apple.com` or `.notify.windows.com`, with the leading dot. Anything else is refused (422 `validation_error` naming `endpoint`) and nothing is stored. The list is in code. Sends do not follow redirects (a `requests.Session` with `max_redirects = 0` passed to `pywebpush`), so a 3xx is a failed send, never a request to another host.

**Sending.** `pywebpush` (`webpush()`, sync) runs on the push module's own `ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=10)` through `loop.run_in_executor()`, with `timeout=3` per send and TTL 3,600 seconds, up to 10 at a time, inside the reminder job's push budget (decision 9). Not `asyncio.to_thread`: cancelling the job does not stop a send already in a thread, and `asyncio.run`, which uvicorn uses, waits on exit for every thread of the loop's default executor, so a send in flight would add its whole timeout after the 5-second stop. `runner.stop()` calls the push module's `shutdown_sends()`, which runs `executor.shutdown(wait=False, cancel_futures=True)` once the job is done or cancelled: queued sends never start, and the loop closes without waiting for the executor. Python still joins that executor's threads when the interpreter exits, so the 3-second timeout is what bounds the process: a send in flight when the shutdown starts ends within about 6 seconds (connect plus one read), inside Docker's 10-second grace. A new executor is made on the next `runner.start()`. The payload is `{"title", "body", "url": "/app/notifications?thought=<id>"}`, which the browser decrypts; the push service sees only the encrypted bytes. A 404 or 410 answer deletes that subscription. Before sending, the step deletes every one of the run's subscriptions whose `key_id` differs from the current public key's, in one `DELETE`, and sends nothing to them: they were made under a key pair the operator has since replaced, every send to them would fail, and without this each reminder would try and log each of them again. Any other failure is logged with the push service's host and status code only, never the endpoint, and is not retried.

**Browser.** `quick-capture` (feature 5, planned in parallel, not on this branch; Context) also plans `public/sw.js`: a worker with `install` calling `skipWaiting()` and `activate` calling `clients.claim()`, registered at scope `/` on every page by `components/pwa/service-worker.tsx`, and served in demo mode through `demoRequest()` with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Whichever change lands second must not overwrite the other's file or its `proxy.ts` rule: one outcome would lose the `push` handler and the same-origin `/app/` check below, a security control; the other would lose quick-capture's install handling. So task 4.4 extends `public/sw.js` when it exists, keeping its `install` and `activate` handlers and adding `push` and `notificationclick`, and adds the file with `activate` alone when it does not; quick-capture, landing second, extends it the same way. The Settings section reuses the registration `getRegistration()` gives when there is one, and calls `navigator.serviceWorker.register("/sw.js", {scope: "/"})` only when there is none. The handlers this change needs:

- `activate` calls `event.waitUntil(self.clients.claim())`, so the tab where the user turned push on, which was loaded before `sw.js` was registered, becomes controlled by it. Without it that tab stays uncontrolled, including after client-side moves to other pages, and `WindowClient.navigate()` rejects with a `TypeError` on an uncontrolled tab.
- `notificationclick` closes the notification and first checks the address: `new URL(data.url, self.location.origin)` is used only when its origin equals `self.location.origin` and its path starts with `/app/`; anything else, a malformed one included, becomes `/app/notifications`. So whoever can push to a subscription (for example with a leaked VAPID private key) cannot open another site from a notification that looks like the app's.
- It then looks through `clients.matchAll({type: "window", includeUncontrolled: true})` for a tab on the app's origin. When there is one, it focuses it and calls `client.navigate(url)`; when `navigate` rejects or resolves to null, it calls `clients.openWindow(url)` instead. With no tab, it calls `clients.openWindow(url)`. Focusing a tab without navigating it would leave the user on whatever page it showed.

It holds no token and makes no request. `/sw.js` must load without a session in every mode. Task 4.4 keeps whichever `proxy.ts` rule for it is already in place (quick-capture's `demoRequest()` handling, when that has landed) and, when there is none, leaves `/sw.js` out of `proxy.ts`'s matcher. The Settings section "Notifications" shows "Browser notifications" only when `enabled` is true, with four states: not supported by this browser, blocked in the browser's settings, on, off. Turning it on asks for permission, reuses or registers the worker, unsubscribes a subscription made under another key, subscribes with the public key, posts the subscription, and writes the marker; turning it off unsubscribes, sends the `DELETE`, and removes the marker. The section reads its state from `navigator.serviceWorker.getRegistration()`, then `pushManager.getSubscription()`, its `applicationServerKey`, and the marker: with no registration it shows "off" at once, and it never waits on `navigator.serviceWorker.ready`.

**Alternatives:** `asyncio.to_thread` for sends (rejected: the default executor is joined when the loop closes, above); a 10-second timeout per send (rejected: a send in flight at shutdown could take the exit past Docker's grace, and push services answer in well under a second); our own RFC 8291 encryption with `cryptography` and `httpx` (rejected: more security code to own than one maintained library); a push relay (rejected: an extra service); store subscriptions encrypted with `encrypt_secret()`, under a hash of the endpoint (rejected: a push needs the VAPID private key, which sits in the environment beside `KEY_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, so encryption stops no one who could push; it would cost a hash column and a re-encryption whenever an endpoint moves to another user); post the subscription again on every Settings load through the moving upsert (rejected: if A turned push on and A's session ended without a sign-out, B opening Settings to change a model would take A's subscription without opting in, so B's reminder titles would show in a browser profile A still uses, and A's would stop); handle `pushsubscriptionchange` in `sw.js` to post a renewed subscription (rejected: Chromium browsers do not fire it, and the worker would post without the page's checks of the user and the key; the restore on each app load covers every browser); keep sending to subscriptions of an old key until the push service answers 404 or 410 (rejected: many answer 401 or 403 instead, so the rows would never go and would fail and log on every reminder); replace quick-capture's `public/sw.js` with this change's own (rejected: whichever lands second would drop the other's handlers); a payload with only an id that the service worker fetches (rejected: the worker would need the session, and a logged-out browser would show nothing); allow any `https` host (rejected: an internal service with TLS is still reachable); a prefix or suffix string check on the URL (rejected: `https://fcm.googleapis.com.evil.example/`, `https://fcm.googleapis.com@10.0.0.5/`, and `evilnotify.windows.com` pass it); a unique `(user_id, endpoint)` so two users can hold one endpoint (rejected: the browser would then show both users' reminders); refuse a POST for an endpoint another user holds with 409 (rejected: it tells the caller another account exists on that browser, and the person now using it could never turn pushes on); `GET ?endpoint=` to ask the server whether this browser is subscribed (rejected: the browser already knows, the marker says for which user, and the restore on load brings back a deleted row); last-sent time on the row (rejected: nothing reads it); store the body as the browser sent it, checking only the endpoint (rejected: keys that cannot encrypt are stored and fail on every send, above, and the row's size is the caller's choice); refuse a body with any key beyond the three (`extra="forbid"`) (rejected: a browser that adds a field to `toJSON()` would silently lose push, and the body is read in full either way, so refusing protects nothing that storing only the three fields does not); a plain `str` for the private key (rejected: `repr(settings)` and error messages would show it); the upsert as a select, then a delete and an insert (rejected: two posts at once fail on the unique endpoint, above); refuse push at start when the offline providers file is used (rejected, above: the keys already turn it off, and the providers file does not prove the host is offline); open `data.url` as given (rejected: any pusher could open any site); `navigator.serviceWorker.ready` for the registration (rejected: it never settles when no worker is registered, so every sign-out on a browser that never turned push on would wait 2 seconds, and the section would stay loading); no `clients.claim()`, with `openWindow` only when no tab is found (rejected: the tab where push was turned on is the one most likely open, and clicking would do nothing there).

### 12. The feed token: one per user, shown once, stored hashed

`calendar_feeds`: `user_id` primary key (cascade), `token_hash TEXT UNIQUE` (SHA-256 hex), `email` (decision 17), `created_at`. The token is `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)` (43 characters, 256 bits).

- `GET /api/settings/calendar-feed` → `{active, created_at}`.
- `POST /api/settings/calendar-feed` → 201 `{path: "/api/calendar/<token>.ics"}`. It makes a new token and replaces any old one in one write, so the old address stops at once. The write is one statement, `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (user_id) DO UPDATE SET token_hash = excluded.token_hash, email = excluded.email, created_at = excluded.created_at`, so two calls at once both answer 201 and leave one row, never a 500 on the primary key; the later write wins. This is the only answer that holds the token.
- `DELETE /api/settings/calendar-feed` → 204, also when there is none.

Settings shows "Calendar feed": when off, "Make a feed address"; when on, "made on <date>", "Regenerate" and "Turn off" (each with a confirmation that says the old address stops working). After a `POST`, the section shows the full address (`window.location.origin` plus `path`) once, with Copy, and says it will not be shown again. There is no `webcal://` link: several common clients (Thunderbird, Outlook, Google Calendar's URL import) turn `webcal://` into `http://`, so on an instance served over HTTPS each poll would send the path, token included, in clear text before any redirect to `https`. The user pastes the `https://` address into the calendar app instead. The section always shows one fixed line, in the off state before the first `POST` and beside the address once made: "Anyone with this address can see the titles, summaries, and categories of your open dated thoughts. Regenerate it if it is shared by mistake." The address works without a login and its events go to whatever calendar service the user subscribes with, so the user is told what it shares before making it. "Make a feed address" and "Regenerate" are disabled while a `POST` is pending, so one section never sends two: with two in flight, the answer that arrives last could hold the token that the other's write already replaced, and the section would show an address that no longer works and is never shown again. In demo mode the section is not shown and all three routes answer 404 `not_found`.

**Alternatives:** a `webcal://` link beside Copy (rejected: the clients above fetch it over plain HTTP and send the token in clear text); a `webcal://` link only when the page is served over `http:` (rejected: it helps only on `localhost`, where pasting works as well, and is a second state to test); no note on what the address shares (rejected: the user would not know that the address alone, with no login, shows their thoughts to anyone holding it); store the token encrypted and show it any time (rejected: a token that can be shown again can leak again; regenerating is one click); a "last fetched" time (rejected: the feature asks for make, regenerate, and turn off, and it would add a database write to every anonymous, rate-limited request); a token per feed with several feeds per user (rejected: no use for more than one); the token in a query string (rejected: same exposure as the path, and some calendar apps drop query strings); a select, then an update or an insert (rejected: two calls at once could both insert and one would fail on the primary key); refuse a second `POST` within a few seconds on the server (rejected: "Regenerate" right after "Make" is a real use, and disabling the button covers the double click).

### 13. The feed request: its own Next.js route, a rate limit, and no token in the log

**Route.** `frontend/app/api/calendar/[file]/route.ts` handles only `GET`. A static segment wins over the catch-all, so `/api/calendar/...` never reaches `app/api/[...path]/route.ts` and its credential. The route checks the file name against `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{43}\.ics$` (else 404 `not_found` without calling the backend), forwards `GET /api/calendar/<file>` to `BACKEND_URL` with no `Authorization`, no cookies, and no request headers, with `redirect: "manual"`, and passes back the status, `content-type`, `cache-control`, `retry-after`, and the body. A fetch that throws, or an upstream 3xx, answers 502 `{"error":{"code":"backend_unreachable","message":"The server could not be reached. Try again."}}`, the same as the catch-all, with no `location` header passed back; it is logged without the path. In demo mode it answers 404 at once. `proxy.ts` needs no change for this route: it lets every `/api/` path through in both modes (in demo mode after its per-address rate limit) without starting a session.

This route is a frontend API route that is called with no session and forwards to the backend with no credential, which `auth-modes`' user-auth requirement "Browser never holds the access token" does not allow as written: it says the frontend server attaches the user's credential to each forwarded call, and that a frontend API route called with no session answers 401 without calling the backend. So the `user-auth` delta also modifies that requirement, naming the feed route as the one exception to both, and saying it forwards no cookie or credential (decision 15).

**Backend.** `GET /api/calendar/{file}` in `app/routers/calendar.py` is the one route with no user dependency. It looks up `sha256(token)`; an unknown token, a malformed name, demo mode, or an owner the email allow-list no longer lets in (decision 17) answers 404 `not_found` with the body every unknown address gets. It writes nothing.

**Rate limit.** `app/rate_limit.py` is a token bucket like `frontend/lib/rate-limit.ts`, in this process's memory, that reads the time from `utc_now()` in `app/thoughts/dates.py`, the one clock the runner and the reminders read, so tests fix it and move it forward: 60 requests per hour per token hash, and 120 per hour shared by all requests whose token is unknown. Over either, 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After`. Calendar apps poll every few minutes to a day, so 60 an hour leaves room. The shared bucket for unknown tokens slows a guess loop without affecting real feeds; guessing 256 bits is out of reach anyway. A name that does not match `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{43}\.ics$` is answered 404 before any database query and counts against the shared unknown bucket; a well-formed name is looked up, and only a known token's hash gets a bucket of its own. So the limiter's map holds at most one bucket per feed that exists, plus the shared one, and a loop of made-up tokens neither escapes the shared limit nor grows the map.

**Caching.** The answer has `Content-Type: text/calendar; charset=utf-8` and `Cache-Control: private, max-age=300`. No `ETag` or 304: a feed of at most 2,000 events, fetched at most 60 times an hour, does not need conditional requests, and the feature does not ask for them.

**Access log.** The access-log rewrite from `thought-library` (which already replaces `args[2]` of each `uvicorn.access` record with the path before `?`) also replaces a path starting `/api/calendar/` with `/api/calendar/[REDACTED].ics`. The Next.js server writes no access log.

A reverse proxy in front of the app is outside this rewrite. The documented setup puts Caddy in front (`docs/login.md`, "HTTPS on your network"), and its sample `Caddyfile` has no `log` directive, so Caddy writes no access log; but a `log` directive an operator adds, or another proxy's default access log, writes the full URI, token included. So `docs/login.md`'s "HTTPS on your network" and the "Calendar feed" section of `docs/notifications.md` each say: if you turn on access logging in the reverse proxy, filter out `/api/calendar/` paths, since the address is the feed's only credential.

**Alternatives:** `ETag` and `If-None-Match` (rejected: work in three layers for a small, rate-limited feed); a `last_fetched_at` write per request (rejected, decision 12); an exception for `calendar` inside the catch-all (rejected: one shared route deciding per path whether to attach a credential is easy to break); a route outside `/api/`, such as `/calendar/<token>.ics` (rejected: the request names `/api/calendar/`, and `proxy.ts` would need a public-page rule); the rate limit in the Next.js route (rejected: the backend is where the token is known, and it is one process); no word to operators about a proxy's own log (rejected: the token would land in a log file the app cannot see, through a setting the docs themselves lead operators to).

### 14. What the feed holds

The feed is built with `icalendar`: `VERSION:2.0`, `PRODID:-//Focus Funnel//Thoughts//EN`, `CALSCALE:GREGORIAN`, `X-WR-CALNAME:Focus Funnel`, `REFRESH-INTERVAL;VALUE=DURATION:PT1H`, and `X-PUBLISHED-TTL:PT1H`. It lists the user's **open** dated thoughts whose last day (or `repeat_until`, or no end for a repeat with none) is no more than 30 days before today in UTC, at most 2,000, latest start first. The filter is `(repeat_every IS NULL AND coalesce(ends_on, starts_on) >= :cutoff) OR (repeat_every IS NOT NULL AND (repeat_until IS NULL OR repeat_until >= :cutoff))`: a filter on `coalesce(ends_on, starts_on)` alone would drop "Take the bins out every Monday" once its first Monday is more than 30 days old, the main repeat case. Each is one `VEVENT`:

- `UID:<thought id>@focus-funnel`, `DTSTAMP` and `LAST-MODIFIED` from `updated_at`.
- `SUMMARY` the title, `DESCRIPTION` the summary, `CATEGORIES` the category when there is one.
- A day with no time: `DTSTART;VALUE=DATE`, `DTEND;VALUE=DATE` the day after the end day (or the start day).
- A time: `DTSTART;TZID=<time_zone>` and `DTEND;TZID=…` when there is an end; no `DTEND` without one. `Calendar.add_missing_timezones()` adds a `VTIMEZONE` for each zone used, so repeats keep their local time across daylight saving.
- A repeat: `RRULE` with `FREQ`, `INTERVAL`, `BYDAY` (weeks), `BYMONTHDAY` (months), `BYMONTH` and `BYMONTHDAY` (years), `WKST=MO`, and `UNTIL` (the date, or for a timed repeat the end of that day in the thought's zone, as UTC).

It holds nothing else: no raw text, tags, reminders, status, user id, email, or link back to the app. A done thought is not in it, as it is not on the Upcoming page.

**Alternatives:** write the iCalendar text by hand (rejected: line folding, escaping, and `VTIMEZONE` are easy to get wrong, and the library is small and pure Python); times as UTC (`Z`) with no `VTIMEZONE` (rejected: a weekly 19:00 would move by an hour across daylight saving); include done thoughts (rejected: the feed follows the Upcoming page); all past events (rejected: the feed would grow without end; 30 days back keeps last month visible).

The delta is `thought-status`' version, with that sentence limited to thoughts that do not repeat, the repeating rule, and two scenarios added. **Archive after `thought-status`.**
- `user-auth` "Backend API requires a valid access token" must name the feed as an exception, and say how the allow-list reaches it (decision 17). The delta is `auth-modes`' version with the feed added. **Archive after `auth-modes`.**
- `auth-modes` "Email allow-list" must change: its scenarios "Address not on the list" and "List changed while a user is logged in" say any API call of a user the list does not let in answers 403 `not_allowed`, and the feed request is an API call, which answers 404 `not_found` with the unknown-address body (decision 17). The delta is `auth-modes`' version with the feed's 404 named as the one exception, the rule that such a user gets no pushes, and the scenario "Calendar feed and pushes of a user no longer allowed". **Archive after `auth-modes`.**

If any base delta changes before it is archived, copy the new version into this delta again.

**Alternatives:** an added requirement that "refines" one below it without copying it (rejected: once both are archived, the main spec would hold two requirements that disagree, and the older one would still say a weekly thought does not pass); leave `auth-modes`' "Email allow-list" as it is, since decision 17 applies the list to the feed (rejected: its scenarios say any API call answers 403 `not_allowed`, and the feed answers 404, so the two would disagree once archived).

### 16. Reading `when` back: the repeat from the columns, reminders in one batched query

The repeat is four columns on the row, so `when_of(row)` writes `repeat` wherever it writes the date (decision 4). Reminders are rows of `thought_reminders`, so `when_of()` cannot read them from the thought. `when_of(row, reminders=None)` adds `reminders` (sorted offsets) only when it is given a list, and `reminders_for(session, user_id, thought_ids) -> dict[UUID, list[int]]` in `app/reminders.py` reads them for many thoughts in one owner-filtered query.

Which answers carry `when.reminders`:

- Every one-thought answer: `GET /api/thoughts/{id}`, every `PATCH` answer (a `when` change, a title-only edit, and `thought-status`' status-only `PATCH` from the sheet's Done toggle alike), and the answer of `POST /api/thoughts/{id}/additions/{addition_id}/undo`. All of them are built by `thought_answer()` in `app/routers/thoughts.py`, so the one `reminders_for()` call for the one thought goes there, and no route builds a `ThoughtOut` another way. Whatever fields a `PATCH` sent, its answer carries `when.reminders` (`[]` for none, never a missing key) whenever the thought has a date. These are what the sheet reads and edits: `ThoughtSheet` loads the thought with `GET` when it opens, then replaces its state with each `PATCH` answer (`thought-editing` decision 7, `thought-status`' Done toggle) and with `undoAddition()`'s answer (`onSaved(restored)`), and fills Edit from that state with no new `GET`. If any of these answers lacked `reminders`, a later move of the start would send `reminders: []` (decision 7) and clear them with no message.
- The export: `thought-export`'s batch reads them with one `reminders_for()` call per batch, inside the batch's REPEATABLE READ transaction after `additions_for()`, so a thought and its reminders come from one snapshot, and the export makes one more query per batch, not per thought. `thought_view()` takes the reminders as one more argument. The JSON export then holds `when.repeat` and `when.reminders`, and the `thought-export` delta adds both to what each thought carries (decision 15). The Markdown `- Date:` line adds the repeat text after the zone (`- Date: 2026-11-09 (Europe/Oslo), repeats every week on Mon`) and a `- Reminders: 60, 1440 minutes before` line when there are any.
- List answers do not: Upcoming items, library rows, search sources, and `target` carry `when` with `repeat` and without the `reminders` key. A list has no use for them, and each would need the second query. The frontend never sends a list's `when` back: the sheet edits only from a one-thought answer, above.

**Alternatives:** call `reminders_for()` in the `GET` and `when`-changing `PATCH` routes only (rejected: the sheet takes the title-only, status-only, and undo answers as its state too, and a later start move from that state would clear the reminders); a `Thought.reminders` relationship with `lazy="selectin"` (rejected: every `select(Thought)` in the app, the search and the similar-thought check included, would run the extra query whether it needs reminders or not, and a `load_only` or column select would still need its own handling); reminders as an array column on `thoughts` (rejected: the job needs a row per reminder with its own indexed fire time, decision 8); leave the export unchanged (rejected: it would drop reminders, or read them one query per thought).

### 17. The email allow-list reaches the feed and push

`auth-modes` says the backend enforces the allow-list on every request, and `check_allowed()` does it from the token's email in `Authenticator.current_user()` (Context). The feed request has no token, and the reminder job has no request, and `users` stores no email. Left alone, a user the operator removed from `AUTH_ALLOWED_EMAILS` would keep a working feed and keep getting pushes.

So the two features that act without a login keep the verified email address they were set up with:

- `calendar_feeds.email` and `push_subscriptions.email` (`TEXT NULL`) hold the address of the identity that made the feed or posted the subscription, lowercased, and only when the provider said it is verified; else null. Today `Authenticator.current_user()` calls `authenticate()` and `check_allowed()` inside and returns only the `User`, and `DemoAuthenticator` overrides it, so the `Identity` never reaches `get_current_user`. So `current_user()` returns `(user, identity)`, in the base class and in `DemoAuthenticator.current_user()` alike, and `get_current_user` sets `request.state.identity` from it and still returns the user; every route keeps its `User` dependency unchanged. The two `POST` routes read the identity from `request.state`. A demo identity has no email, so it would store null; demo mode has no feed or push anyway. "Turn on" and the restore (decision 11) update it.
- When `AUTH_ALLOWED_EMAILS` is set, the feed route answers 404 `not_found`, the unknown-address body, for a feed whose `email` is null or not allowed by `email_allowed()`; the reminder job sends no push to a subscription whose `email` is null or not allowed. The in-app notification is still made; the user cannot read it while the list keeps them out. The feed answers 404, the unknown-address body, rather than the 403 `not_allowed` every other call of such a user gets, so the answer says nothing about whether the address was ever valid; the `auth-modes` delta names this one exception in "Email allow-list" (decision 15).
- With no list set, and in demo mode, the address is not checked, as for every other request.

The address is never in the feed, a push payload, or a log. It goes with the feed or the subscription when they are deleted, and with the account.

**A user removed at the provider.** Without `AUTH_ALLOWED_EMAILS`, the default, an operator usually ends someone's access by disabling them in Pocket ID or Firebase. The app hears nothing of that: the feed token and the push subscription need no login, so that person's feed keeps answering and their reminders keep being pushed until the account is deleted. `docs/notifications.md` (in its push part and its "Calendar feed" section) and the "Allow-list" section of `docs/login.md` say so: to end a removed person's feed and pushes, have them delete their account (Settings, "Delete account") before their login is disabled, or set `AUTH_ALLOWED_EMAILS` without them and restart. There is no operator command to delete another user's account, and this change adds none.

**Alternatives:** a second method, `current_identity()`, called by `get_current_user` beside `current_user()` (rejected: it would verify the token twice per request, and an override could check one and not the other); call `authenticate()` again in the two `POST` routes (rejected: a second verification, and in demo mode a second lookup, for data the first one had); name the feed and push as exceptions to the allow-list, with a docs note that removing someone does not revoke their feed until the account is deleted (rejected: an operator who removes an address expects access to end); a `users.email` column kept up to date on each request (rejected: a write on the login path that `auth-modes` does not plan, and an email stored for every user, where only feed and push owners need one); look the address up at the provider (rejected: there is no token to ask with); record the last verified login on the feed and subscription rows and stop serving or pushing after a stated period without one, such as 90 days (rejected: a write on the login path for two features, and a user who stays signed in on one device for months would lose their feed for no reason they can see; the docs note and the allow-list cover the operator who removes someone).

### 18. Deleting data

"Delete content" deletes the user's thoughts, which cascade their reminders and the notifications that name them, and `delete_user_content()` also deletes the user's other notifications (`thought_id` null, for later kinds). It keeps the calendar feed and the push subscriptions, which are settings: the feed then answers with no events. "Delete account" removes all four tables' rows by the cascade from `users`.

The confirmations in `components/delete-data-settings.tsx` stay fixed strings and gain this change's data. "Delete content": "Your filed thoughts and their search index, their reminders and your notifications, and your conversations and their messages, are deleted for good. Your account, API keys, models, categories, usage history, calendar feed, and browser notifications stay." "Delete account" names reminders, notifications, the calendar feed, and browser notifications among what is deleted.

Each task group that adds one of these tables changes the deletes and adds its data to both strings in the same pull request (tasks 3.9, 4.8, and 5.7): group 3 reminders and notifications, group 4 browser notifications, group 5 the calendar feed. So the strings read as above once group 5 ships, and no release in between says less than it deletes or keeps.

**Alternatives:** one last group that changes both confirmations after groups 3 to 5 (rejected: groups 3 and 4 would ship deletes the confirmations do not name, which `stop-and-spend-cap` does not allow); delete the feed with the content (rejected: a calendar app subscribed to it would get 404 and keep stale events, where an empty feed clears them; the feed is a setting, like the models); leave the confirmations as they are (rejected: `stop-and-spend-cap` requires them to say what is deleted and what stays).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A reminder is up to about a minute late: a 30-second job, a 30-second sleep at most] → Accepted for reminders set in minutes. The interval is one number in the job list.
- [A crash between the commit and the push loses that push] → Accepted: the in-app notification is there, and a doubled push is worse than a lost one.
- [A user with more than 1,000 repeating thoughts] → Upcoming reads the first 1,000 by start; the rest are found by search. Not expected for a brain dump.
- [Months without the 31st, and 29 February] → Skipped, as calendar apps skip them. The repeat text says "on day 31", and the Upcoming page shows the real next occurrence.
- [Calendar apps that ignore `VTIMEZONE` or `REFRESH-INTERVAL`] → The `TZID` is an IANA name, which the large clients read; refresh timing is up to the client.
- [The feed address leaks (a shared screenshot, a synced calendar)] → It shows titles and summaries of dated thoughts only; "Regenerate" or "Turn off" ends it at once.
- [Push services change hosts] → The list is in one place; a refused subscription says the browser's push service is not supported, and in-app notifications still work.
- [The token passes through the Next.js server's memory and the calendar app's storage] → Unavoidable for a subscribable feed. It never reaches the access log, the database, or the browser after the one answer.
- [A second backend process started by mistake] → Jobs are claimed in the database, and notifications have a unique key, so nothing is sent twice; `background-turns` still rules the second process out.
- [A job that hangs] → `asyncio.timeout` per job (60 seconds by default, 90 for `reminders`), logged as a failure.
- [A push service is down or slow] → The push step has its own 20-second budget and sends 10 at a time; the rest are dropped and counted in the log. The in-app notifications are there, and the next run's batches are not delayed.
- [One user with hundreds of reminders at the same minute] → Each of their browsers gets at most 5 pushes and an "and N more" push per run, after every other user's first notification; the in-app list has them all.
- [A browser used by two accounts] → A subscription belongs to the last user who turned pushes on there, and signing out unsubscribes it. A browser closed without signing out keeps the previous user's pushes until the next user turns them on; opening the app or Settings never moves it (decision 11). The docs say to turn them off before handing a browser over.
- [The operator changes the VAPID keys] → Every stored subscription stops for good. The sender deletes them without trying, Settings shows Off in each browser, and each user turns push on again; the docs say so (decision 11).
- [A browser renews its subscription on its own] → The next app load restores it; until then, reminders reach the in-app list only.
- [A person disabled only at the identity provider] → Their feed keeps answering and their reminders keep being pushed until their account is deleted or the allow-list is set without them; the docs say so (decision 17).
- [A reverse proxy's own access log] → It would hold the feed token. The sample `Caddyfile` has none, and the docs say to filter `/api/calendar/` out of any that is turned on (decision 13).
- [A feed or subscription made before the allow-list was set, by a provider that does not say the email is verified] → It stops working once the list is set, as that user's login does. Making it again after logging in fixes it when the address is allowed.
- [A `PATCH` on a thought whose reminder the job is sending] → It waits for that batch's commit, a fraction of a second; a thought delete, "Delete content", "Delete account", and the demo cleanup never deadlock with the job (decision 9).
- [The Postgres container restarts while the backend runs] → The runner logs `job runner error` and tries again 30 seconds later (decision 2); reminders due meanwhile go out late, within the 24-hour catch-up.
- [Two tabs make or regenerate a feed address at once] → Both answer, the later write wins, and one tab shows an address that no longer works; "Regenerate" fixes it. One section never sends two (decision 12).

## Migration Plan

1. One Alembic revision per task group that adds data, chained in group order on top of the head left by the changes below this one (keep the single-head test passing): group 1 `scheduled_jobs` (`name`, `next_run_at`, `running_since`); group 2 the four nullable repeat columns and the check constraint on `thoughts` (batch mode); group 3 `thought_reminders` (no occurrence columns) and `notifications`; group 4 `push_subscriptions` (`endpoint` unique, `p256dh`, `auth`, `key_id`, and `email` in plain columns, no last-sent time); group 5 `calendar_feeds` (with `email`, no last-fetched time). No backfill: no thought repeats or has reminders yet.
2. The task groups can ship one at a time, as separate pull requests. The runner (group 1) ships with an empty job list; it is harmless alone (it wakes every 30 seconds and claims nothing), and group 3 adds the first jobs. Group 5 needs group 4's `(user, identity)` change in `app/auth.py` (decision 17), so it ships after group 4. Each of groups 3 to 5 ships its own delete changes and confirmation text (decision 18). Old frontends send `when` without `repeat` or `reminders`, which stores neither, and ignore the new keys in answers.
3. New dependencies: `python-dateutil` (group 2), `pywebpush` (group 4), `icalendar` (group 5). The offline bundle picks them up from `uv.lock`.
4. Rollback: downgrade drops the columns and tables. Repeats, reminders, notifications, subscriptions, and feeds are lost; the dates stay. A calendar app subscribed to the feed gets 404 and keeps its last copy.
